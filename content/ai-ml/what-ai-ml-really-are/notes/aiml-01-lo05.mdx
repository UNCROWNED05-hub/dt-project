### Core Idea
Gradient Descent is an iterative optimization algorithm that updates parameters $\theta$ in the direction of steepest descent—the negative gradient of the cost function:
$$\theta \leftarrow \theta - \alpha \nabla_\theta J(\theta)$$
Where $\alpha$ is the **learning rate**, a positive scalar tuning the step size. For MSE with respect to weight $w$, the partial derivative is:
$$\frac{\partial J}{\partial w} = \frac{2}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (\hat{y}_i - y_i) x_i$$

### Worked Example
One step of gradient descent for a single parameter $w$:
- Current weight: $w = 2.0$
- Learning rate: $\alpha = 0.05$
- Computed gradient: $\frac{\partial J}{\partial w} = +4.0$ (loss increases as $w$ increases)
- Update step:
$$w_{\text{new}} = 2.0 - (0.05 \times 4.0) = 2.0 - 0.2 = 1.8$$
Weight correctly decreases to move toward the loss valley.

### Common Mistake
Setting learning rate $\alpha$ too high, which causes gradient descent to overshoot the minimum and diverge toward infinity; or setting $\alpha$ too small, taking millions of iterations to converge.

### Check Yourself
If the gradient $\frac{\partial J}{\partial w} = 0$, what does the update rule do? ($w$ does not change; a local or global stationary point has been reached).
