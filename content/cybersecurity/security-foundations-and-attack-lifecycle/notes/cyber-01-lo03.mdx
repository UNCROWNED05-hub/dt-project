### Core Idea
An **Attack Surface** is the sum total of all reachable points where an unauthorized entity can attempt to enter or extract data. An **Attack Vector** is the specific pathway or mechanism used to exploit a weakness.
Key vectors include:
- Phishing and social engineering (human layer)
- Unpatched external-facing web applications (software layer)
- Exposed management ports like RDP/SSH to the public internet (network layer)
- Misconfigured cloud storage buckets and weak IAM permissions (cloud layer).

### Worked Example
Reducing attack surface for a production web server:
- Initial state: SSH (port 22) open to 0.0.0.0/0, database (port 5432) open to public, web app (port 80, 443).
- Hardened state: Close port 5432 to public, move port 22 behind a zero-trust bastion VPN with hardware MFA, enforce HTTPS on 443, close port 80. Attack surface reduced by 75%.

### Common Mistake
Believing an unused service or closed port has zero risk. If an orphaned daemon is listening on an internal interface without updates, an attacker who establishes a foothold can pivot to it immediately.

### Check Yourself
What is the difference between an attack surface and an attack vector? (The attack surface is the totality of exposed endpoints; the attack vector is the specific vehicle or exploit path used to breach it).
